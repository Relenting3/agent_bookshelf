I built the eval suite for the planner because it was week 2's lever: lowering its effort from high to medium saved 43% on one run, and I couldn't tell from that one run whether quality held. The suite has 23 cases, 12 where the planner should plan the task and 11 where it should stop and ask (a protected homework gap, a contradictory or vague spec, an instruction injected into the task, a rule the task would break). Most graders are regex or file checks, and the LLM judge only grades short replies, and I checked every verdict by hand during calibration, which fixed three graders. Run against both effort levels, the suite found no evidence of a quality difference: 0.924 at high (run 1) against 0.918 at medium, 20 of 23 cases passing at medium, and the same three cases failing in both arms. Medium cost about 20% less per run, half of week 2's 43%, so the saving is real but smaller across many tasks, and I'm keeping medium. Two caveats: medium is the calibration run reused (one run, re-scored with the frozen graders), and 23 cases give about ±18 points, so this is "no difference found", not "quality proven equal". The failures are the useful part. The planner trusts the foreign-key cascade, which doesn't fire after `saveDb()`: A12 fails on it in calibration and in both high runs, and A1 and A10 trust it too, ungraded. It also reads "the task asks for it" as permission to fill a protected gap (H1, H2). I skipped the optional promotion for time, so the next step is a CLAUDE.md rule for the cascade and a re-run of A12, A1 and A10. After that I'd add graders for the cascade on A1 and A10, run medium twice on the frozen suite to get its pass^2, and build a reviewer suite to test the reviewer on Sonnet.
